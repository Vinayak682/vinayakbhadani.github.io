Taking on selected GCC planning work

Three problems I take on.

Most planning problems in the GCC are not modelling problems. They are a forecast nobody trusts, a seasonality model that breaks every year, or a monthly meeting that reports instead of deciding. These are the three I take on, each scoped, each ending in something you can act on rather than a deck.

Engagements

Forecast Accuracy Diagnostic

2 weeks

You have an accuracy number and no agreement on what it means. This decomposes it properly — at category–month level, separating bias from variance — so you can see whether you have a forecasting problem, a demand-shaping problem, or a data problem. Most teams find it is not the one they assumed.

What happens

  • MAPE and bias decomposed by category, month and lead-time bucket
  • Baseline separated from promotional and calendar effects
  • Accuracy measured at the level you actually order on, not the level that flatters it

What I need

  • 24–36 months of shipment or sales history
  • Your current forecast, as published
  • One hour each with planning and commercial

You end with a single accuracy number you can defend, the three changes that move it most, and the order to do them in.

Lunar-calendar seasonality rebuild

3–4 weeks

Ramadan drifts about eleven days earlier every year. Holt-Winters, SARIMA and the seasonality components inside most ERP demand modules learn a pattern indexed to a fixed calendar position — so they learn that March is a high month, and then quietly break. This rebuilds seasonality against the actual event, not the month it last landed in.

What happens

  • History re-indexed to lunar events rather than Gregorian months
  • Ramadan, Eid, Hajj and National Day treated as separate effects per market
  • Pre-Ramadan build, in-month collapse and post-Eid recovery modelled apart

What I need

  • Three or more years of history spanning a drifting Ramadan
  • Which GCC markets you sell into
  • Access to whoever owns the ERP forecast settings

You end with a seasonal index set that survives the drift, and the reasoning written down so the next planner does not undo it. The method is published in full — read it before you decide.

S&OP cadence redesign

4–6 weeks

The meeting exists, attendance is good, and nothing gets decided in it. That is almost never a discipline problem — it is that the cadence presents numbers instead of forcing trade-offs, and the decisions that matter have no owner and no deadline attached.

What happens

  • The monthly cycle rebuilt as a decision forum with named owners
  • Each meeting given a specific trade-off it exists to resolve
  • Escalation paths defined for the calls that cannot be made in the room

What I need

  • To sit in on one full cycle before changing anything
  • The current pack and agenda
  • A sponsor who can make attendance and ownership stick

You end with a written cadence, a decision log that outlives the engagement, and a first cycle run with me in the room.
