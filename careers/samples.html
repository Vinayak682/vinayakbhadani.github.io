Summary
Inventory coordinator with 2 years in retail distribution. I build the weekly stock-cover report store managers use to reorder, and reconcile system stock against physical counts. Moving into demand planning; I've completed a forecasting and S&OP program and published a practice forecast model.
Experience
  • Built the weekly stock-cover report in Excel (PivotTables, XLOOKUP) from SAP exports, so 12 store managers could see which items would run out before the next delivery.
  • Reconciled SAP stock against monthly cycle counts and traced each gap to its cause (in transit, returns, unposted transfers), bringing open discrepancies down from about 60 lines a month to under 20.
  • Sent the buying team a monthly list of items more than 90 days old, which they used to plan markdowns.
Projects
Practice demand forecast on a public retail dataset: moving average vs. exponential smoothing, scored with WAPE and bias.

What changed, and what didn't

  • Each bullet now says what she did, with which tool, and who used it.
  • Every number is one she can explain: "12 store managers" is a headcount; "60 to under 20" comes from her own monthly discrepancy log.
  • Keywords from demand planning JDs (forecast accuracy, WAPE, bias, S&OP, stock reconciliation, SAP) appear only where they're true. Tools she hasn't used, like Kinaxis or IBP, stay out.
  • No employer, title or date was added or changed.

The review checklists

Resume review checklistAbbreviated public version
  • Verification first: every employer, title and date matches something you can prove; every number can be explained ("How did you calculate that?"); unverifiable roles are removed, not reworded.
  • Targeting: one version per role family; the top third answers "why this person for this role" in six seconds.
  • Format and ATS: 1 page (2 only with 5+ years); text-based PDF or .docx; no text boxes, columns, icons or skill bars; standard headings; consistent dates; file named Firstname-Lastname-Resume-Role.pdf.
  • Summary: target role + real years + 2-3 strengths + core tools; no filler like "hard-working" or "results-driven".
  • Bullets: action → what → number → result; 3-5 per role; nothing you couldn't talk about for two minutes.
  • Keywords by role family (demand planning, inventory, supply chain analyst, procurement, logistics, tools), used only where true. List SAP, Oracle, IBP, Kinaxis, o9 or Blue Yonder only if you've actually used them.
  • Projects listed as projects, never disguised as jobs: problem → data → method → result → link.
  • Certifications "in progress" only if you're actually enrolled.
  • Final check: matches LinkedIn exactly; you can explain each bullet out loud in under 60 seconds.
LinkedIn review checklistAbbreviated public version
  • Consistency first: employers, titles and dates match your resume exactly. Recruiters cross-check.
  • Top card: clear photo, a supply chain banner, a headline of target role + 2 strengths + tools (not "Seeking opportunities"), location set to where you want to work, a custom URL, Open to Work filled in.
  • About: three short paragraphs: what you do → proof (real experience and projects) → what you're looking for.
  • Experience: 2-4 bullets per role, same substance as the resume; company pages linked.
  • Featured: your best project or portfolio pinned first.
  • Skills: top 3 match the target role; trim the rest.
  • Activity: one post a week about something you learned or built.
