If you remember one thing

The job is a six-step weekly loop

Every responsibility in the JD is one of these steps. If you can explain them in your own words, you understand the role.

  1. Check the numbers (PSI)

    Pull the week's sales, purchases and stock for every account and product. Spot what moved.

  2. Clean and shape demand

    Fix data errors, tag one-off promotions, adjust for known events, all before the weekly deadline.

  3. Agree and load the forecast

    Agree the numbers with the sales and account teams, add the safety buffer, and load the forecast into the planning system.

  4. Publish the weekly report

    What changed since last week, what's at risk, and what it means in dollars against the sales plan.

  5. Match supply to the plan

    Check factory arrival dates, reconcile channel inventory, and confirm or adjust purchase orders.

  6. Stop stock from getting old

    Find aging stock, plan how to sell it down, and move it to where it sells faster.

The business in one picture

Follow one phone from factory to customer

The factory has to decide how many to build weeks before anyone buys. Too few means empty shelves and lost sales. Too many means phones losing value in a warehouse. Demand planning exists to get that number right.

60–90 second pitch (a template)

  1. Your background in one sentence, and where you worked with data or operations.
  2. Why planning: a real reason connected to your experience.
  3. What you've done to learn it: courses, projects and practice you've actually completed.
  4. What you'd bring to this team's weekly cycle.

Real experience only. If you're asked about something you haven't done, say so plainly and explain how you'd approach it. Invented details fall apart under the second follow-up question, and employment history gets checked.

Five questions to ask them

  1. How many forecast versions does the team run each week, and who owns each one?
  2. Which accounts or product lines would I support first?
  3. How does the team measure forecast accuracy and bias today?
  4. Which systems and data sources does the team use day to day?
  5. What does a great first 90 days look like in this role?
