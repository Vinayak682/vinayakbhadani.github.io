      Interview lab

      Skeletons, not scripts.

      Real questions from demand planning and ERP interviews, anonymised. Each one shows what’s being tested, the order to answer in, and the follow-up to expect. You fill in your own true story.

      22 QUESTIONS
      Demand planningTell me about a time you organised and ran a recurring process.+

      What they’re testing

      Whether you can run a deadline-driven cycle with structure, owners and checkpoints.

      Answer skeleton

      1. Agree the definition first (e.g. what counts as ageing)
      2. Build one shared view, refreshed on a fixed day
      3. Fixed rhythm: refresh → analysis → review → actions
      4. Each action gets an owner and due date
      5. Result with one real number

      If they push

      “How did you prioritise?” — value at risk: units × cost × closeness to the threshold.

      Demand planningWhat experience do you have with customer segmentation?+

      What they’re testing

      Whether you treat accounts differently by value and behaviour.

      Answer skeleton

      1. Segment by value (ABC) to decide review depth
      2. Segment by behaviour (steady vs volatile)
      3. Read channel ordering patterns — batch orders aren’t growth
      4. Say what changed in how you forecast each group

      If they push

      If you never ran formal ABC/XYZ, say “a simple value and volatility split”. Still a strong answer — and true.

      Demand planningHow have you managed a demand planning system?+

      What they’re testing

      Clean data, on-time loads and reconciled numbers.

      Answer skeleton

      1. Extract sales, inventory and orders
      2. Reconcile before every update
      3. Update and load before cut-off
      4. Publish one view for the business

      If they push

      “What do you reconcile?” — system on-hand vs reported, sell-in vs shipments, sell-through vs POS, week-over-week totals.

      Demand planningTell me about a time you struggled to build consensus on a forecast.+

      What they’re testing

      Holding a data-based position without being rigid.

      Answer skeleton

      1. Show sell-in vs sell-through and weeks of supply
      2. Ask what Sales knows that the data doesn’t
      3. Add a dated, specific uplift — not a blanket increase
      4. Agree a weekly review trigger

      If they push

      “Leadership overrules you?” — load the agreed number, document the risk in quantity and value, track it weekly. The target is what we want; the forecast is what we expect.

      Demand planningGive an example of a mistake you made with data.+

      What they’re testing

      Honesty, fast ownership and the control you added afterwards.

      Answer skeleton

      1. What broke (e.g. a lookup missed a changed product code)
      2. How you caught it — totals didn’t reconcile
      3. Correct, re-send, tell the people affected
      4. The permanent control you added

      If they push

      Other real mistake shapes: units vs cases, a pivot filter left on, duplicates after a join. Pick one that actually happened.

      Demand planningWalk me through your end-to-end forecasting process.+

      What they’re testing

      A clear, ordered process you’ve clearly done many times.

      Answer skeleton

      1. Collect → Clean → Baseline
      2. Add known events as separate uplifts
      3. Consensus with Sales
      4. PSI check: supply, weeks of supply, buffer
      5. Load, publish, measure accuracy and bias, adjust

      If they push

      “Which step matters most?” — cleaning. A good model on dirty history still gives a bad forecast.

      Demand planningHow do you build, maintain and adjust a forecast?+

      What they’re testing

      The three verbs, separately.

      Answer skeleton

      1. Build: clean history, baseline, dated events
      2. Maintain: weekly roll, refresh actuals, PSI
      3. Adjust: evidence-based triggers only — never one bad week
      4. Every adjustment has a written reason

      If they push

      “Sales wants a big increase on Friday before cut-off?” — ask for the driver, load what’s supported, log the rest as upside risk with a review date.

      Demand planningWhich tools do you use — and how do you use AI?+

      What they’re testing

      Excel depth first, then systems, BI, automation and AI with controls.

      Answer skeleton

      1. Excel: PivotTables, XLOOKUP, conditional flags
      2. Automation: macros for the weekly refresh
      3. BI dashboards for forecast vs actual
      4. AI: drafts commentary and debugs formulas — you validate

      If they push

      “Weeks of supply formula?” — ending inventory ÷ average forward weekly sell-through.

      ERP & master dataWhat is an item master, and why does one wrong field hurt every team?+

      What they’re testing

      Whether you see master data as shared infrastructure.

      Answer skeleton

      1. Define it: the ERP’s ID card for a part
      2. Who uses it: buy, store, plan, build, cost
      3. Your control: a validation template before entry

      If they push

      “Released product?” — created at shared level, then released to a legal entity where company-specific defaults live.

      ERP & master dataWalk me through setting up a new part.+

      What they’re testing

      An ordered checklist from approved source to tested evidence.

      Answer skeleton

      1. Start from the approved drawing / request
      2. Check for duplicates
      3. Basics: UoM, groups, tracking
      4. Purchasing, inventory and planning defaults
      5. Link BOM and routing if made
      6. Test transaction, save evidence

      If they push

      “Required field missing?” — don’t guess. Hold it inactive, return to the owner, log it.

      ERP & master dataWhat goes wrong with units of measure?+

      What they’re testing

      Whether you know the most common silent ERP error.

      Answer skeleton

      1. Bought in boxes, used in eaches
      2. Missing conversion = MRP orders 100× too much or too little
      3. Standardise and define conversions

      If they push

      Ask: “Where do most data issues come from today?”

      ERP & master dataEngineering BOM vs manufacturing BOM?+

      What they’re testing

      Design intent vs how it is actually built.

      Answer skeleton

      1. EBOM: the product as designed (PLM)
      2. MBOM: adds packaging, consumables, scrap, operation grouping
      3. Classify every difference: intentional vs error

      If they push

      “Phantom?” — a sub-assembly not stocked separately; its components go straight into the parent order.

      ERP & master dataAn ECO changes a component on an assembly with open production orders. What do you do?+

      What they’re testing

      Controlled change, not editing live data.

      Answer skeleton

      1. Read ECO: change, revision, effectivity, disposition
      2. New BOM version from the effectivity date; end-date the old
      3. Agree treatment of released orders with planning
      4. Old stock: use up, rework or scrap; stop buying it
      5. Test with an estimate; record evidence

      If they push

      No approved ECO, no change.

      ERP & master dataHow do you validate PLM-to-ERP data?+

      What they’re testing

      Reconciliation discipline across systems.

      Answer skeleton

      1. Export both sides
      2. Compare key fields: part, revision, component, qty, UoM
      3. Classify differences; errors go back with evidence

      If they push

      “Which is right?” — released PLM for design, but some MBOM differences are intentional. Confirm before copying.

      ERP & master dataA production order won’t estimate or release. How do you troubleshoot?+

      What they’re testing

      Systematic root-cause thinking.

      Answer skeleton

      1. Item released to that site and not blocked?
      2. Active, approved BOM and routing versions for that date?
      3. Components released and active?
      4. UoM conversions and default warehouse?
      5. Work centre capacity?

      If they push

      Fix the master data, not just the one order.

      ERP & master dataMRP suggests a huge purchase order nobody expected. What do you check?+

      What they’re testing

      Whether you can trace a planned order to its cause.

      Answer skeleton

      1. Trace planned order to its demand
      2. Unit conversion, BOM qty or scrap %
      3. Safety stock, coverage rule, MOQ
      4. Stale lead time or double-counted demand

      If they push

      Fix the data, rerun, confirm with the planner.

      ERP & master dataEngineering needs a part released today, but the drawing isn’t approved.+

      What they’re testing

      Whether you protect the control under pressure.

      Answer skeleton

      1. Prepare the record, keep it inactive
      2. Flag timing risk to engineering and planning
      3. Escalate if it threatens the build

      If they push

      Releasing unapproved data saves an hour and can cost much more later.

      FundamentalsInventory is a two-sided risk. Explain.+

      What they’re testing

      Whether you balance service and cash.

      Answer skeleton

      1. Stock-out: lost sales, churn
      2. Excess: tied-up cash, obsolescence, space
      3. Name a cause and response for each

      If they push

      Pair turns with fill rate — never optimise one alone.

      FundamentalsHow would you assign ABC classes?+

      What they’re testing

      Value thinking, not unit counting.

      Answer skeleton

      1. Annual value = volume × unit cost
      2. Rank and cumulate
      3. Set explicit thresholds and document them

      If they push

      The lowest-volume SKU can drive the most value.

      FundamentalsDaily demand 40, lead time 5 days, safety stock 60, on hand 180, on order 30, backorders 10. Order today?+

      What they’re testing

      Reorder-point arithmetic, said out loud.

      Answer skeleton

      1. ROP = 40 × 5 + 60 = 260
      2. Position = 180 + 30 − 10 = 200
      3. 200 < 260 → reorder

      If they push

      Curveball: supplier adds 2 days → 80 units of extra lead-time demand. Expedite, allocate to top customers or find alternate supply.

      FundamentalsIs 95% forecast accuracy good?+

      What they’re testing

      Whether you define a metric before praising it.

      Answer skeleton

      1. Which formula — MAPE or WAPE?
      2. At what level — SKU, family, total?
      3. What is the bias?

      If they push

      A strong company-wide average can hide a recurring shortage on a flagship SKU.

      FundamentalsWho attends S&OP and what do they bring?+

      What they’re testing

      The cross-functional picture.

      Answer skeleton

      1. Sales & Marketing: intelligence, promotions
      2. Operations & Procurement: capacity, constraints
      3. Finance: targets, margin, cash
      4. Supply chain leadership: scenarios, escalation

      If they push

      Output: one approved plan with recorded decisions and owners.
